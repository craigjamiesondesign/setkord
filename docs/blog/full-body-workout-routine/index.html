Full Body Workout Routine: The Complete Guide and Weekly Plan

2 September 2026 · Mark Stent

  • programs
  • training

A full body workout routine trains every major muscle group in a single session, run two to three times a week with a rest day between each. Each workout has a lower-body push, a lower-body pull or hinge, an upper-body push, an upper-body pull, and a little accessory work - so the whole body gets hit every time you train. It is the best split for anyone training two or three days a week, because it keeps every muscle at high frequency even on a light schedule.

What is a full body workout routine?

Instead of splitting the body across the week, you cover it all in one session. Every full-body workout should contain:

  • A squat or knee-dominant lift - squat, leg press, lunge.
  • A hinge or hamstring lift - deadlift, Romanian deadlift, hip thrust.
  • An upper-body push - bench press, overhead press, dip.
  • An upper-body pull - row, pull-up, lat pulldown.
  • Accessories - a couple of smaller movements for arms, shoulders, or calves.

That is five or six exercises that together touch every major muscle. Train three times a week and each muscle gets three exposures a week - frequency a body-part split can only dream of.

The full body weekly plan

A three-day full-body week, with the main lift rotated each session so nothing gets stale:

DayMain lowerMain upper pushMain upper pullAccessories
MonSquatBench pressBarbell rowCurls, calves
WedDeadliftOverhead pressPull-upTriceps, abs
FriFront squatIncline benchLat pulldownLateral raise, calves

Rotating the main lifts (squat / deadlift / front squat, bench / press / incline) means each muscle is trained three times a week but never the exact same way three times, which keeps recovery manageable and hits it from more than one angle.

Full body on two days a week

If two days is all you have, full body is still the right answer - it is the only split that keeps every muscle at a usable frequency on so few sessions:

DayFocus
MonFull body A (squat-led)
ThuFull body B (deadlift-led)

Two hard full-body sessions a week will build real strength and size for a beginner or anyone maintaining. A body-part split on two days would leave most muscles trained once every week or worse - which is why frequency, not fancy exercise selection, is the point here.

Sets, reps, and volume

Because you hit each muscle multiple times a week, you only need a few hard sets per muscle per session - often two to four - to reach a productive weekly total. Three sessions of three sets lands each muscle around 9 to 12 hard sets per week, comfortably inside the range covered in training volume for muscle growth and how many sets per week to build muscle.

Reps are ordinary: 5 to 8 on the heavy compounds, 8 to 15 on accessories. The frequency does the heavy lifting, not exotic rep schemes.

Who the full body routine is for (and who it isn't)

Full body is a great fit if you: train two or three days a week, are a beginner or returning lifter, have an unpredictable schedule (missing one day costs less when every day hits everything), or just want maximum results from minimum sessions.

Look elsewhere if you: can train four or more days and want more volume per muscle - an upper/lower split or push pull legs will use those extra days better - or you specifically want a heavy barbell-strength focus, where a 5x5 (itself a full-body style plan) fits the bill.

FAQ

Is a full body workout good for building muscle? Yes. Training each muscle two or three times a week is excellent for growth, and full body is the simplest way to get that frequency on few sessions. As long as your weekly hard sets are in range and you progress, it builds size and strength well.

How many days a week should I do a full body routine? Two or three, with a rest day between sessions. Full body is specifically the best-fit split for that range. If you can train four or more days, a split that hits each muscle across separate days usually uses the extra time better.

Can I do a full body workout every day? No. Every muscle needs recovery, so back-to-back full-body sessions dig a fatigue hole. Leave at least a day between them - three sessions a week is the practical ceiling for this style.

Full body or a split - which is better for beginners? Full body, in most cases. Beginners progress fastest on high frequency and simple sessions, and full body delivers both. Move to an upper/lower or PPL split later, when you can train more days and want more volume.
