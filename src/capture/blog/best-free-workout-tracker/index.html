The Best Free Workout Tracker: What to Look For (and Honest Picks)

2 September 2026 · Mark Stent

  • comparison
  • apps

The best free workout tracker is the one that lets you log every session, keeps your personal records, and does not hold your own data hostage behind a paywall. Plenty of apps call themselves free and then cap you at a handful of workouts, hide your history, or make you pay to get your data back out. A genuinely free tracker gives you a complete, usable log for nothing and only charges for the deeper extras. Setkord is built exactly this way, so it is the one I will make the case for, but I will be honest about what "free" really means first.

What "free" actually means

"Free" hides a lot of different things. Before you commit your training to an app, check three things:

  • Is logging capped? Some free tiers limit how many workouts, routines or exercises you can save. A tracker that stops you logging is not really a log.
  • Can you get your data out? If there is no CSV export, your history is trapped. A good free app lets you leave with your numbers.
  • What is actually behind the paywall? Fair apps charge for depth - advanced analysis, exports, extra history. Unfair ones charge for the basics you need every session.

If an app passes those three, it is worth your time. If it fails the first two, the "free" label is doing a lot of heavy lifting.

What a good free tracker needs

You do not need much to train well, but you do need these to be reliable and free:

  • Fast set logging that does not fight you between sets.
  • Automatic personal records, so you know when you have beaten your best.
  • An estimated 1RM per lift, so you can see strength trending up.
  • Reusable routines, so you are not rebuilding your session every time.
  • A way to import your history and export it again.

Everything past that - deep charts, plateau flags, PDF exports - is a nice-to-have, and it is fair for an app to charge for it.
