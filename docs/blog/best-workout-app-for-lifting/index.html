The Best Workout App for Lifting: Built for Lifters, Not Everyone

2 September 2026 · Mark Stent

  • comparison
  • apps

The best workout app for lifting is one built strength-first: it logs a working set in a tap, tracks your personal records and estimated 1RM automatically, and shows you volume and muscle balance so you can actually program your training. Most fitness apps try to do everything - steps, meals, meditation, a bit of lifting - and the lifting ends up as an afterthought. If you are serious about the barbell, you want an app that treats strength as the main event. That is exactly what I built Setkord to be.

What serious lifters actually need

Strip away the noise and a good lifting app comes down to a handful of things you use every single session:

  • Fast logging. Between sets you have seconds, not minutes. Entering weight and reps should be a tap or two, with last time's numbers already there.
  • Automatic PRs. You should not have to remember your best - the app should tell you the instant you beat it.
  • Estimated 1RM. A single number per lift that tells you if strength is trending up, without maxing out.
  • Routines and programs. Your training plan, saved, so you start a session ready to go instead of building it from scratch.
  • Volume and muscle balance. Enough analysis to see how many hard sets you are doing per muscle and where you are neglecting.

Notice what is not on that list: social feeds, calorie counters, guided meditations. Nice for some, but not why you lift.

Strength-first is a design choice, not a slogan

Being built for lifters shows up in the small stuff that a general fitness app never bothers with:

  • Warm-up sets are flagged so they never inflate your working volume or steal a PR.
  • Supersets and giant sets are grouped with clear on-screen separation, so alternating stays as fast as a straight set.
  • Dumbbell handling lets you enter the combined weight you lifted while volume is always tracked as true load.
  • A plate calculator shows exactly what to load per side of the bar, in kg or lb.
  • Per-exercise rest overrides and an automatic rest timer that keeps running when your phone locks.

These are the details you only build if lifting is the point, not a tab.

FAQ

What is the best app for weightlifting? For strength-first training, look for fast logging, automatic PRs, estimated 1RM, routines and volume tracking. Setkord is built around exactly those, and its core is free.

How does an app estimate my 1RM? It uses your logged weight and reps to estimate the most you could lift for a single rep, so you can track strength trending up without ever testing a true max. Setkord charts an estimated 1RM for every lift.
